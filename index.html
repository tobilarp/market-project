How to read this

Everything here is an ETF

Not one row is an index, a barrel of oil or a currency. Each is a fund that trades like a share and tracks one of those things. SPY isn't the S&P 500 — it's a fund holding the 500 constituents. The Tracks column names the exposure; the Ticker is what actually trades.

Why "unusual" beats "big"

Natural gas routinely swings 3% in a day; long-dated Treasuries doing the same would be a serious event. Comparing every instrument against its own history is what makes those two comparable.

What a z-score is

A count of standard deviations. A z of 2 means today's move is about twice as far from that asset's average day as its typical wobble — roughly a 1-in-20 session if returns were well behaved, which they aren't quite.

Sector rotation, plainly

Money moving between parts of the market rather than in or out of it. Technology leading while utilities lag is a different market from both falling together, even if the index closes flat.

Why one day misleads

A run of small daily moves in the same direction adds up to something a single day never shows. Crude read roughly flat for three sessions at the start of September while quietly sitting 3.6% above where the week began. The cumulative columns exist because of that — and they compound the prices rather than summing the percentages, since +5% then −5% leaves you down, not level.

What a flag is worth

It says "look here", nothing more. An earnings date, an index rebalance and a genuine dislocation all leave the same statistical footprint. Separating them is a judgement, which is what the analyst note is for.

Methodology

How a move gets flagged

A collector records each symbol's closing quote every weekday. The daily percentage change is scored against the mean and standard deviation of that symbol's own prior daily changes: z = (today − mean) / stdev. A reading of |z| ≥ 2 is flagged. Scoring needs at least 10 prior observations; below that the screen reports insufficient data rather than a number it can't support.

Why ETF proxies

The free data tier covers equities but not futures or FX, so commodities and foreign indices are tracked through liquid ETFs that hold or follow them — USO for WTI crude, GLD for gold, EWU for UK equities, FXB for sterling. These carry tracking error and, for the commodity funds, roll cost, so they are directional indicators rather than spot prices.

Known limits

Daily closes only, so intraday moves are invisible. The z-score assumes returns are roughly normal — a convenient approximation rather than a true one, since real return distributions have fatter tails, so |z| ≥ 2 fires somewhat more often than the textbook 5%. And nothing here knows why anything moved.
