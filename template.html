    Methodology

    How a move gets flagged

    Each trading day a collector records the closing quote for every symbol on the watchlist. A symbol's daily percentage change is scored against the mean and standard deviation of its own prior daily changes: z = (today − mean) / stdev. A reading of |z| ≥ 2 is flagged — the move is roughly two standard deviations from how that asset normally behaves, which is the definition of "anomalous" used here. Scoring requires at least 10 prior observations; below that the screen reports insufficient data rather than a number.

    Why ETF proxies

    Finnhub's free tier covers equities but not futures or FX, so commodities and foreign indices are tracked through liquid ETFs that hold or track them — USO for WTI crude, GLD for gold, EWU for UK equities, FXB for sterling. These carry tracking error and, for the commodity funds, roll cost, so they are directional indicators rather than spot prices.

    Known limits

    The baseline is built from daily closes only, so intraday moves are invisible. A flag is a prompt to investigate, not a signal — an earnings date or an index rebalance produces the same statistical footprint as a genuine dislocation, and separating those requires reading the news, which is why headlines sit alongside the screen.
